# Vocabulary acquisition inside a topic

Read this file whenever a self-study topic, practice session or worksheet introduces or
continues vocabulary. It defines how a word moves from a correct visible model to productive
recall. `topic-materials.md` owns the surrounding topic flow. Controls assess the result and
never teach through this cycle.

Instructions are English. Learner-facing headings, prompts and answers remain Russian.

## Evidence and project decisions

Research findings used here:

- Retrieval with feedback improves delayed comprehension and production of foreign words
  compared with imitation, without a pronunciation penalty
  ([Kang, Gollan & Pashler, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23681928/)).
- Reviews of adult L2 vocabulary learning support retrieval and spaced encounters, while
  finding no single optimal number or interval for every learner and word
  ([Seibert Hanson & Brown, 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/review-of-laboratory-studies-of-adult-second-language-vocabulary-training/18F0A5D1FFC829CE05931B2EEE83124A)).
- Written forms help learners acquire spelling and spoken word form; the evidence for
  meaning is positive but less uniform
  ([orthographic-facilitation review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31600470/)).
- Handwriting can strengthen lexical orthography when the later task also requires written
  production
  ([Bosse et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24575058/)); a newer child study
  also found benefits for word form, sound and meaning
  ([Yang et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38703657/)).
- Thai reading and spelling do not develop identically; spelling may lag reading and Thai
  readers use larger lexical or syllabic units as well as letter-level information
  ([Winskel & Iemwanthong, 2010](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Reading-and-spelling-acquisition-in-Thai/991012821882802368)).
- Repeated encounters across varied contexts can promote deeper processing, but findings
  are mixed and context diversity is not a substitute for deliberate retrieval
  ([Chandy, Serrano & Pellicer-Sánchez, 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/effects-of-contextual-diversity-on-incidental-vocabulary-learning/F83A1EB55404E2E07FDC1695B0BC2BAE)).
- A direct replication and mini meta-analyses did not confirm a general immediate-learning
  advantage for longhand lecture notes over laptop notes
  ([Weinstock et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33539228/)).

Project decisions derived from that evidence and the learner's stated preferences:

- use handwriting because the learner finds the act of writing useful and later needs Thai
  written production, not because all handwritten notes are universally superior;
- show an accurate complete form before hiding it and asking for recall;
- distribute meaningful encounters and retrievals instead of fixing one exposure count or
  one schedule for every word;
- keep notebook blocks compact and accurate for occasional rule lookup;
- treat recognition, spelling recall, contextual use and later return as complementary
  evidence rather than one universal mastery threshold.

Thai-specific studies do not establish one best drill for adult Russian-speaking learners.
They constrain the script work; the retrieval, feedback and spacing mechanics come from the
broader L2 evidence and adapt through the project tracker.

## Choose a coherent word group

Teach a small thematic group that fits the current coherent part of the topic. Make it
smaller when spellings are long or easily confused. Do not enforce a universal word count.

Choose from:

1. words needed for the current situation or construction;
2. new words in the source's logical order;
3. due or previously weak words that fit naturally.

The topic may show a full vocabulary overview first, but an overview is orientation, not a
lesson or successful review. Spiral words do not replace active practice of the new group.

## Word-learning actions

Use these actions as a repertoire, not a mandatory difficulty ladder. Their order and
frequency depend on the word and teaching part.

### Complete visible model

Present the link once in a short, notebook-friendly form:

```text
тайское слово · кириллическая транскрипция — русское значение
Короткая естественная фраза: [фраза]
```

Keep the whole Thai word visible during first inspection. A fully new word is being taught,
so this exposure receives no tracker quality.

### Useful spelling map

For a difficult form, mark only what helps reconstruct it:

- syllable-sized chunks that visibly recombine into the whole word;
- the first unstable letter or vowel pattern;
- a contrast with the form the learner confuses;
- one reliable anchor tied to meaning and spelling.

Do not reduce every word to an isolated letter list, invent an etymology or pile several
mnemonics onto one spelling.

### Hidden productive recall

After the model has been hidden, cue the word from Russian meaning, a picture or a short
situation. In paper material, require handwriting in some of these tasks. After an error:

1. show the complete correct word;
2. point to the first relevant difference only;
3. close or cover the answer;
4. reproduce it;
5. return to it later after other meaningful work.

Copying a visible answer is exposure. It can support encoding but is not evidence of
recall. A normal miss during initial teaching uses `record ... --item-only` only when there
was an actual response; it does not create a mistake pattern or reopen the topic.

### Changed context and direction

Return to the word after intervening items and vary the useful action: recognise its
meaning, supply it in a simple phrase, reconstruct it from partial support, or use it in a
new bounded situation. Recognition contributes evidence but does not prove exact spelling.

Avoid mechanical repetition. Vary contexts when they remain clear and natural. A word
printed passively in a story counts as an encounter, not a successful retrieval.

### Later return

Schedule later work through the tracker and the project spiral. Harder words return sooner;
easy words leave the immediate queue but still receive a later productive check. Do not
replace the adaptive tracker with a fixed calendar or a universal number of successful
attempts.

## Self-study and assessed modes

Inside a teaching topic or remediation file, every exercise has a hidden prepared answer
under `topic-materials.md`. The file gives the answer and a short explanation after the
learner opens it; it does not grade, diagnose or create another remediation block.

In a live learning session, reveal feedback after the learner responds. In an ordinary
worksheet, keep keys separate or hidden according to its purpose. In a formal control,
omit models, spelling maps, scaffolds and answer keys entirely.

## Tracker use

Record only an actual response. Judge the exact action asked for. A productive Thai spelling
without visible support earns stronger evidence than recognition; copying or assembling a
fully supplied form is weak evidence. Use the existing 0–5 quality definitions in
`progress-and-spiral.md` rather than a second vocabulary-only scale.

During first learning, use `--item-only` for weak performance so the word returns without
shaking the whole topic. Create a `lexeme` mistake through `thai-mistakes` only when the word
fails again in a later spaced attempt or assessment, or the learner says it systematically
does not stick.

## Coverage audit

Before a topic is complete, compare its full target vocabulary with all active exercises.
Every word must be actively recalled, selected by meaning or used in at least one task. A
table or worked example does not count. Spread further meaningful encounters across later
parts and reviews whenever immediate repetition would be artificial.

Do not call a word learned from recognition alone. Use the combination relevant to that
word: unaided recognition, productive recall and spelling, use in a new context and a later
return. Topic closure and long-term scheduling remain owned by the existing tracker and
closing-test rules.

## Checklist

1. The word group is small and coherent for this part, without a fixed quota.
2. Meaning, pronunciation, full Thai form and a natural context are accurate.
3. Complex spelling has only the reconstruction support it needs.
4. The correct model appears before hidden recall.
5. Short handwritten actions are distributed through the topic.
6. Encounters and task types vary without mechanical repetition.
7. Self-study exercises have hidden keys; formal controls have none.
8. Passive exposure is not recorded as successful review.
9. First-learning misses use item-level tracking; recurring exact-word failure uses
   `lexeme` remediation.
10. The final coverage audit finds no vocabulary item left only in a list or example.
